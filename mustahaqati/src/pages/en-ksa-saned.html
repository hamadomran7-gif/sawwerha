Calculate your SANED benefit

Basic plus housing.

SANED at a glance

MonthsBenefitMonthly cap
1–360% of the average wageSAR 9,000
4–1250% of the average wageSAR 7,500

Example: average wage SAR 8,000 and 12 months without work. Months 1–3: SAR 4,800 a month; months 4–12: SAR 4,000 a month. Total SAR 50,400.

With an average of SAR 20,000, 60% is 12,000, cut to the 9,000 cap, and 50% is 10,000, cut to 7,500, so the total is 3 × 9,000 + 9 × 7,500 = SAR 94,500.

The average wage used

Average wage = total contributory wages (basic plus housing) over the last 24 months ÷ 24. A recent pay rise therefore counts only partly, and an average of SAR 15,000 or more reaches both caps (60% × 15,000 = 9,000 and 50% × 15,000 = 7,500).

Who qualifies

  • Saudi nationals under 60 who contribute to the unemployment branch.
  • Enough contributions: 12 months in the last 36 for the first claim, then 18 in 36 for the second, 24 in 36 for the third, and 36 in 48 after that. A claim counts as new each time the 12 benefit months are used up.
  • You did not leave by choice (resignation) and were not dismissed for a reason attributable to you.
  • You register with the competent authority, look for work seriously, and apply within 90 days of leaving work.

When payments stop

  • When you earn income from work or become unable to work.
  • When you refuse training, or leave the Kingdom for more than 60 days.
  • When the 12 months are used up, or you reach 60 and qualify for a pension.

The 12 months may be continuous or broken, within 24 months of the first payment. SANED is funded by 0.75% of your wage and 0.75% from the employer. It does not replace your end-of-service award, so work out both when your contract ends.
