Before dividing: the order of claims

Funeral costs come first, then the deceased's debts, then a bequest to a non-heir up to one-third, and the rest goes to the heirs (Article 198 of the Saudi Personal Status Law). The division below is of this net estate.

Step 1: list the heirs and drop those excluded

  • A son or son's son excludes all siblings, uncles and their sons.
  • The father excludes the grandfather and the siblings. The mother excludes the grandmothers.
  • An inheriting descendant, the father or the grandfather excludes the maternal siblings.
  • A full brother excludes a paternal half-brother. Two daughters exclude a son's daughter unless a son's son is with her.
  • The nearer residuary excludes the further one: a brother before a nephew, a nephew before an uncle.

Step 2: give the fixed shares

ShareWho gets it
One-halfHusband with no inheriting descendant; one daughter; one son's daughter; one full sister; one paternal half-sister
One-quarterHusband with an inheriting descendant; wife or wives with none
One-eighthWife or wives with an inheriting descendant
Two-thirdsTwo or more daughters, son's daughters, full sisters or paternal half-sisters
One-thirdMother with no descendant and fewer than two siblings; two or more maternal siblings
One-sixthFather or grandfather with a descendant; mother with a descendant or two siblings; grandmother(s); son's daughter with one daughter; paternal half-sister with one full sister; one maternal sibling

Step 3: give what is left to the residuaries

A residuary takes what is left after the fixed shares, or the whole estate if there are none. The order is son, son's son, father, grandfather, siblings, their sons, then uncles. A daughter with a son, or a sister with her brother, inherits two to one. A full or paternal sister with daughters becomes a residuary taking what is left.

Step 4: 'awl or radd

'Awl: if the fixed shares add up to more than the estate, every share shrinks in proportion, dividing by the sum of the shares. Radd: if something is left and there is no residuary, it returns to the fixed-share heirs other than the spouses in proportion to their shares.

Step 5: split into whole parts

Put all the shares over one common denominator; that denominator is the number of parts and each heir gets a whole number of them. If a group's share does not divide evenly among its members, multiply the denominator until it does.

Worked examples

1) Wife, son and daughter, estate 240,000: the wife takes one-eighth because there is a descendant, and the remaining 7/8 goes to the son and daughter two to one. In 24 parts: wife 3 (30,000), son 14 (140,000), daughter 7 (70,000).

2) Husband, mother and father ('Umariyya): the husband takes one-half, the mother one-third of what is left (one-sixth), and the father the rest. In 6 parts: husband 3, mother 1, father 2.

3) Husband, two full sisters and mother ('awl): one-half, two-thirds and one-sixth add up to 8/6, so the case rises from 6 to 8 parts: husband 3, sisters 4 (2 each), mother 1.

4) Wife, mother and daughter (radd): one-eighth, one-sixth and one-half leave 5/24 with no residuary, returned to the mother and daughter one to three, not to the wife. In 32 parts: wife 4, mother 7, daughter 21.

5) Daughter, son's daughter and full brother: the daughter takes one-half, the son's daughter one-sixth to complete two-thirds, and the brother the rest. In 6 parts: daughter 3, son's daughter 1, brother 2.

6) Grandfather and full brother: under Saudi law the grandfather excludes the brother and takes everything (Art. 212). Under Zayd ibn Thabit's view, followed by the majority, he shares with him as a brother, half each.

What the short method does not cover

An unborn child, a missing person, an intersex heir, several people dying together, dividing among distant kin, and the obligatory bequest for the children of a son or daughter who died before their parent where the law provides one. A person who killed the deceased intentionally does not inherit, and there is no inheritance across religions. The final division is the competent court's, through the heirship deed.
