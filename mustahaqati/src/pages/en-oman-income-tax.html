How Oman's personal income tax works

  1. Gross income: everything you receive in the year in cash or in kind: salary, allowances, bonuses, pension, end-of-service gratuity, rent, dividends and interest.
  2. Threshold: if gross income does not exceed OMR 42,000, there is no tax and no return.
  3. Taxable income: gross − 42,000 − costs (15% of rent and self-employment, or actual) − exemptions.
  4. Tax: 5% of taxable income, with no bands.

The Tax Authority says about 99% of the population is below the threshold and will not pay the tax.

Examples for 2028:
An Omani earning OMR 4,000 a month with residential rent of 6,000 and main-home loan interest of 2,000: gross 54,000, deductions 5,600 (rent cost 900 + pension contributions 2,700 + interest 2,000), taxable 6,400 and tax OMR 320.
An expatriate earning OMR 5,000 a month: taxable 18,000 and tax OMR 900 (75 a month), falling to 675 with school fees of 4,000 and medical costs of 500.
An expatriate who works 8 months on 4,500 and receives a gratuity of 25,000: gross 61,000 and tax OMR 950; without the gratuity there would be no tax.

Returns and withholding

  • Anyone whose gross income exceeds OMR 42,000 files an electronic return within 6 months of the year end (first deadline 30 June 2029) and pays with it.
  • Employers withhold tax from salaries expected to exceed the threshold, deducting only exemptions that are part of the salary, such as pension contributions; you recover the rest through your return.
  • Anyone leaving Oman for good files a return at least 60 days before their residence ends.
  • Late payment costs 1% a month of the unpaid amount.
