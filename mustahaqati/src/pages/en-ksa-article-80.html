The nine cases in Article 80

As a rule the employer may not terminate without paying the award, giving notice or compensating. The exception is nine exhaustive cases, provided the employee is given a chance to state the reasons for objecting:

CaseExtra condition
1. Assaulting the employer, the responsible manager, a superior or a subordinate during or because of work—
2. Breaching essential obligations, disobeying lawful orders, or deliberately breaking announced safety instructionsDespite a written warning
3. Proven bad conduct or an act against honour or integrityProven
4. A deliberate act or omission intended to cause the employer material lossReported to the authorities within 24 hours of learning of it
5. Using forgery to obtain the jobProven
6. Employee on probationDuring probation (maximum 180 days)
7. Absence without a valid reason for more than 30 days in a contract year or more than 15 consecutive daysWritten warning after 20 days, or 10 consecutive days
8. Exploiting the job position unlawfully for personal gainProven
9. Disclosing industrial or commercial secrets of the workProven

What is lost and what remains

  • Lost: the end-of-service award for the whole service, notice compensation, and Article 77 compensation.
  • Remains: pay due up to the last working day, and pay for unused annual leave.

Example: actual wage SAR 10,000 and 5 years 10 months of service.

Full award under Article 84 = 5 × 5,000 + 0.83 × 10,000 ≈ SAR 33,333.
If an Article 80 case is proven: zero.
If it is not proven or no chance to object was given: the full award, with notice compensation, and possibly Article 77 compensation.
