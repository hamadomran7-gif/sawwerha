The seven cases in Article 81

The employee may leave without notice and keep all statutory rights in these cases:

  1. The employer fails to meet its essential contractual or statutory obligations to the employee.
  2. It is proven that the employer or its representative deceived the employee about the terms and conditions of work at contracting.
  3. The employee is assigned, without consent, work fundamentally different from what was agreed, beyond what Article 60 allows.
  4. Violent assault or indecent conduct by the employer, a family member or the responsible manager towards the employee or a family member.
  5. Cruel, unjust or insulting treatment by the employer or the responsible manager.
  6. A serious danger at the workplace threatening the employee's safety or health, which the employer knows of and does not act to remove.
  7. The employer's conduct pushes the employee into appearing to end the contract (constructive dismissal).

What you are owed

  • The full award under Article 84, without the Article 85 fractions, even with under two years of service.
  • No notice compensation owed by you.
  • Your pay up to the last day, and pay for unused leave.

Example: actual wage SAR 8,000 and 2 years 9 months of service; the employer has not paid wages for three months despite written demands.

As a resignation: one third only ≈ SAR 3,667.
Under Article 81: the full award = 2.75 × 4,000 = SAR 11,000, plus the unpaid wages.
